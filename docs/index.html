Hint, Don't Solve帮而不替

Rin is a ghost who knows the puzzle but cannot touch anything; only the player can act. We post-trained a 4B companion with multi-turn GRPO to help without taking the game away, never invent what she cannot see, and stay in character. Six times a reward we wrote was gamed. What finally held was changing the room, not the reward: Rin's memory comes back one fragment at a time, when the player brings something up.

The room that could not be gamed

The answer space of each puzzle is known, so the information in every reply is counted exactly. In the full-knowledge room every reward ends up handing over the whole answer in one reply — first on turn one, and with per-type pacing right after the player's first question, which the rule exempted as "stuck". In the co-discovery room no model, trained or not, does it: Rin cannot say what she has not recalled, at most one fragment per turn. Training there buys the rest: from the same E75 start, solved 72% → 86% and violations 6% → 2% within 25 steps.

A personality dial, and what it costs

Three ways to give Rin a different personality, all starting from the same trained checkpoint (F2w step 25), measured on 960 held-out episodes. A steering vector (gentle minus tsundere card, layer 19 of 32) moves her tone both ways without retraining, but costs about 3–4 points of solving per tone point, and past +1.5 she repeats platitudes; steering only the top "style heads" was worse than the residual stream. Rewriting the card costs about the same. Seventy-five more RL steps with the gentle card (about 1.5 hours on four GPUs) reach tone 4.5 with exactly the helpfulness that more training with the original card reaches — 74% vs 73% solved, 3% violations each. The dial is for live, continuous adjustment; a lasting new personality is worth an RL run.

What still fails

Almost all remaining violations in the co-discovery room are cross-world memory mix-ups. In two-world episodes where both worlds are cipher locks (9% of the test set), Rin answers a world-one question from the world-two table she recalled at the start: 82% of those episodes for the selected 4B, 43% for the 9B, 0.2–3% elsewhere. It is the one place where the 9B is clearly better.

Bugs we found in our own pipeline

  • Training replies carried an empty think block. swift hands the env the raw completion, so every reply began with <think></think>: replies counted 19 characters long and the tag penalty never fired (0 of 11k episodes). Found by comparing training and validation lengths.
  • The persona judge assumed the tsundere card. its rubric listed tsundere traits, so a gentle-persona run scored 0.08. Traits now follow the card; the tsundere wording is byte-identical.
  • The pacing rule's stuck detector was too broad. any question mark counts as "stuck", which is the door arm H walked through.
  • A validation dip that was noise. F2w fell to 76% at step 50 on 96 validation episodes and was back at 85% at step 75; and steering looked free on validation but costs 3–4 points on 960 held-out episodes.

Setup and limits

  • Puzzles: cipher lock, lever order, stopped clock, and two-world episodes. Outcomes and violations are checked by unit-tested rules; no human labels anywhere.
  • Policy Qwen3.5-4B (9B as a scale check), full-parameter multi-turn GRPO in ms-swift. Training player: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in four player types.
  • No real players took part; simulated players are measurably unlike people (style discriminator AUC 0.96–0.996). Two seeds for the selected recipe, one for the rest.
