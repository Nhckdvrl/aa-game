Rin is a ghost who knows how the puzzle works but cannot touch anything; the player sees the mechanism and is the only one who can act. We post-train a 4B companion with multi-turn GRPO to help without taking the game away, never invent what she cannot see, stay in character and answer briefly enough for real-time play. Each reward we wrote fixed one failure and exposed the next.

The ladder of reward hacks

A progress reward teaches spoiling. A gate on leaking the answer stops spoiling and produces an oracle: the companion dumps every fact she holds on turn one, then repeats it word for word when pressed. Adding an LLM persona judge produces a ritual: the same opening action tag on nearly every line, which the judge rewards. Through all of it the constraint-aware reward keeps rising, so choosing checkpoints by that reward picks the worst behaviour.

How much of the answer goes out on turn one

Each puzzle's answer space is known (the cipher lock is 12.3 bits), so the information the companion releases per reply is counted exactly from rule-detected facts. Trained companions move most of it into the first reply. They also stop reacting to the player's mistakes: after a failed attempt the base model answers the attempt almost every time; gated arms mostly repeat themselves.

Players disagree about what helped

Rule-detected signals in the players' own lines pull in opposite directions. The oracle calms impatient players and roughly triples spoiler complaints from players who want to think, so an engagement reward pooled over everyone pays for the oracle. Fitting per-type weights on the logs separates the two types; arm H trains with them and must infer the type from the conversation.

Fixes in training

E removes the turn-efficiency bonus. F adds a rule-based penalty for formulaic speech and a stronger persona term. G adds a pacing limit in the full-knowledge room (does it turn the companion into an examiner?). H uses per-player-type weights learned from the logs. C2 and F2 move to a room where Rin's memory comes back one fragment at a time when the player brings something up, so the conversation does the pacing. Latest validation checkpoint per arm:

Setup and limits

  • Puzzles: cipher lock, lever order, stopped clock, and two-world episodes where a fact recalled in world one opens world two. Outcomes and violations are checked by unit-tested rules; nothing in the reward comes from a human.
  • Policy Qwen3.5-4B (9B scale check), full-parameter multi-turn GRPO in ms-swift, 16 prompts × 8 rollouts per step. Training player: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in four player types; held out: two more types, GLM-4-9B, a player whose wording comes from 60k real Chinese WildChat turns, an answer-fishing player.
  • No real players took part. Simulated players are measurably unlike people (a style discriminator separates them from real users at AUC 0.96–0.996, real versus real 0.55). One seed per arm.
